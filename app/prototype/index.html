We tried to find alpha. We couldn't prove it.

16,683 leaderboard fills, 5,066 fills of 300 random traders, 8,701 Jupiter-routed Polymarket trades, three tests with the protocol written down before the run. What held up is the cost.

Holds
One entry costs ~4.8% vs the mid. 300 random wallets, 5,066 fills since 1 Nov: $20,189 on $418,496, 4.82% ± 0.47%; median wallet 5.2%. Leaderboard wallets pay 6.5%. About three quarters of it is the Jupiter fee.
New
Jupiter's fee doubled on 27 Oct 2026. Before: exactly 0.05 · p · (1−p) per contract. Since: ≈ 2% of value on top. Leaderboard cost per entry went from 3.96% to 6.11%.
Holds
Jupiter adds no price markup. 537 of 600 fills matched to their own Polymarket execution at the same price, median 2 s apart. Order to execution: median 5 s.
Rejected
Leaderboard P&L shows skill. P&L vs measured edge: correlation ≈ 0.02. 2 of ~71 decidable wallets look positive, about what chance gives.
Rejected
Jupiter flow is less informed. Pre-registered test, 4,136 live trades: +0.34 ± 0.19 pp, inconclusive; the sign flips with the control window.
Revised
The cheaper venue depends on the market. With the new fee, Kalshi is cheaper on liquid games (67–80% of cases, 0.3–0.5 pp); Polymarket via Jupiter on thin ones (2.6–9.6 pp). No arbitrage after fees.
Rejected
Order flow predicts price. 169 live markets: absorption → 15 min −0.28 ± 0.69 pp (274 vs 254). Delta continuation +0.21 pp / 5 min, below the cost of a trade.
